World-model error falls as autonomous play grows

Increasing autonomous play from 0.54 to 5.11 hours lowers 50-step rotation error by 18.8%, ending 26.4% below a static baseline.

A diffusion play policy covers more transitions

With similar imitation error, the diffusion policy covers 84.2% of transitions versus 55.4% for the deterministic policy.

Questions about DSP

What counts as play here?

Play is goal-free interaction that keeps the object moving, letting behavior cloning learn useful motions without rewards or targets.

Why not demonstrate the task directly?

Play is easier to demonstrate, and with the same 15 minutes of human time DSP reaches 91.4% at 0.6 rad versus 34.4% for goal-conditioned BC.

Why not reinforcement learning?

PPO needs 344M simulator frames to reach 86.7% at 0.6 rad, while DSP reaches 91.4% with 701K frames and avoids unsafe hardware exploration.

Why use the play policy to propose actions?

The play policy supplies feasible contact motions, outperforming an equally fast orientation random walk at 0.6 rad (38.3% versus 11.4%).

Does DSP use simulation?

Not on hardware; Isaac Lab is used only to evaluate baselines in repeatable simulation.

What are the limitations?

DSP is limited by its play repertoire, requires per-step planning compute and initial human play, and has only been shown on cube reorientation and Push-T.

Could a large pretrained behavior model replace the human play?

Possibly: a pretrained behavior model could replace initial human play before autonomous data collection, but we have not tested it.

Will the code and data be released?

Yes; code, data and training details will be linked at the top of this page.
